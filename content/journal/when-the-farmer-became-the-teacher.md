Kaaya began with an admission: we didn't know much.

We came to the village wanting to grow food, keep cattle and save seed, and soon found that none of it could be learned from a screen. How to choose the best seed. What a cow eats in which season. Which plant to give her when she falls ill. This knowledge lives with the people who practise it every day, passed down through generations and kept alive by doing.

So we stepped aside and let the village teach.

### The seed journey

Children followed a seed from the farmer's point of view: how the best seed is chosen, how it is nurtured, and how one seed becomes many, saved for the next season.

### The soil journey

They looked at the soil beneath them, the rocks and the mountains around them, and the long ecological history of the land they were standing on.

### The milk journey

They spent a day beside a cattle farmer: which trees to visit for fodder, green fodder and dry, cleaning the shelter, when to give water, when to bathe the animals. Raising cattle is like raising a child of your own. Slowly the children, and their teachers, saw the rhythm that has to be kept, day after day, for milk to reach our tables. The same rhythm brings food to our plates.

That is how our rural appreciation programme began. And that is when we understood something simple: the farmer is a teacher, and a teacher should be paid for sharing what they know.

We are still learning from them.
